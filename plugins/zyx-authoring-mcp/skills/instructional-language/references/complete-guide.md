# Complete Reference Guide: English Instructional Prose

Read this file only when authoring full OER chapters, multi-section textbooks, or assignments requiring deeper Cognitive Load, Coh-Metrix, Metadiscourse, or OER/TILT standards.

---

## 1. Cognitive Load Theory & Scaffolding Progression

- **Working memory limit:** Novice college readers hold roughly 4 chunks in working memory when learning biologically secondary academic skills. Dense prose forces trial-and-error search that exhausts working memory before schemas form.
- **Worked-Example → Completion-Problem → Fading:** Start with full worked examples (modeling every reasoning step aloud), transition to completion problems (partially solved), and fade guidance only after core schemas stabilize (*Expertise Reversal Effect*).
- **Three simultaneous scaffolds:** Combine *conceptual* scaffolds (what factors matter), *strategic* scaffolds (how to evaluate an argument or solve a problem), and *metacognitive* scaffolds (self-check prompts that catch comprehension illusions).
- **Spatial contiguity:** Place explanatory text directly beside diagrams, tables, or equations to eliminate split-attention.

## 2. Discourse Structure, Coh-Metrix, and Connectives

- **Coh-Metrix five dimensions:** Optimize for *Narrativity* (concrete human-scale framing), *Syntactic Simplicity* (short active clauses, low dependency distance), *Word Concreteness* (Dual Coding on core ideas), *Referential Cohesion* (verbatim noun/argument/stem overlap across adjacent sentences), and *Deep Cohesion* (explicit causal and temporal links).
- **Connective polarity rule:** On difficult texts, **contrastive** (`however`, `although`, `but`) and **causal** (`because`, `therefore`, `as a result`) connectives significantly improve comprehension; students misguess negative-polarity causal/contrastive links nearly 50% of the time when omitted. Conversely, stacked **additive** connectives (`furthermore`, `moreover`) degrade comprehension.
- **Rhetorical patterns:** Compare-contrast and problem-solution structures outperform flat descriptive or chronological lists for low-working-memory college readers. Use one clean rhetorical pattern per section.
- **Micro-chunking & Signaling:** Break chapters into 300 to 500 word subsections with descriptive headings, bold <= 20% of words (core terms and anchors only), and insert brief consolidation pauses ("Try It" checks).

## 3. Lexical Bar, Glossing, and Plain Language

- **Tier 2 & Tier 3 Lexical Bar:** Watch both polysemous academic Tier 2 words (`approach`, `diminish`, `coherent`) and discipline-specific Tier 3 terms. Explicitly flag *false familiarity* terms whose everyday meaning differs from their technical meaning (`model`, `theory`, `adaptation`, `significant`).
- **Glossing hierarchy:** Prefer interactive/self-check glosses, inline hyperlinked/tooltip glosses, or marginal definitions right beside the paragraph over end-of-book glossaries alone.
- **Plain Language & WCAG 2.1 (3.1.3 to 3.1.5):** Eliminate hidden verbs (`make a decision` → `decide`), noun sandwiches (`student enrollment data analysis report` → `report analyzing student enrollment data`), double negatives, and culture-bound idioms.

## 4. Personalization, Visible Author, Metadiscourse, and OER / TILT Standards

- **Personalization + Task Relevance:** Pronouns (`you`, `your`, `we`) activate Social Agency and improve transfer, especially for low-prior-knowledge learners, **only when paired with task involvement** (explaining why the concept matters to a task the reader must perform).
- **Hyland Metadiscourse:** Balance *interactive* markers (frame markers `First`/`Next`, endophoric pointers `See Table 2.1`, code glosses `in other words`) with *interactional* engagement markers (inclusive `we`, gentle directives `Notice`/`Compare`, and guiding questions).
- **Visible Author:** Let the tutor's reasoning appear transparently ("Here is why I check the units before plugging in numbers") in a warm, respectful tone that supports belonging without sacrificing perceived expertise.
- **OER & TILT Architecture (OpenStax, BCcampus, Rebus, TILT Higher Ed):**
  - Measurable learning objectives (Bloom verbs) at section start.
  - Modular 300 to 500 word blocks with inverted-pyramid paragraphs.
  - Consistent Style Sheet for terms and notation.
  - Distinct pedagogical callouts (Worked Example, Try It self-check with explained answer, Key Takeaways).
  - Assignments and practice tasks structured with **Purpose** (why it matters), **Task** (exact steps), and **Criteria for Success** (what good work looks like).
